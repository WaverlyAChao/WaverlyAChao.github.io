Four Years of Building Robots—and a Team

I progressed from builder to Head of Build and Team Lead while driving in competition all four years. Along the way, I led the full robot build and electrical integration, helped grow our roster from five to fifteen students, and guided the team from missing Super Qualifiers to a strong Championship performance.

Mechanical Design Electrical Systems Fabrication Team Leadership Outreach

My role

I spent four years on B.3.V.R., taking on increasing technical and organizational responsibility each season. As Head of Build in sophomore year and Team Lead in junior and senior years, I directed the entire build process from early concepts through competition-ready integration.

I approved designs, coordinated work across the team, built mechanisms, completed the robot’s electrical system, documented progress, contributed to match strategy, and served as a competition driver throughout all four seasons.

Engineering process

With limited resources, we focused on making our process more deliberate and repeatable. I helped move the team toward clearer design reviews, faster physical iteration, improved documentation, and better coordination between mechanical, electrical, and strategy work.

Team growth

I helped develop B.3.V.R. from a five-person group into a full fifteen-member team. That required more than recruiting: I created a structure that distributed responsibility, helped newer students contribute, and kept technical and outreach work moving toward shared deadlines.

Outreach & teaching

I led and supported outreach initiatives designed to make engineering visible and approachable to younger students and the broader community.

  • Hosted interactive robotics booths at B-STEAM events, block parties, and other community programs.
  • Created curricula and taught after-school robotics classes for lower-school students, including groups of up to 30 third- and fourth-grade girls.
  • Designed and taught the inaugural Robotics Summer Intensive, helping middle-school students build a competition-ready FTC robot in one week.
  • Mentored students through prototype development and presentations while encouraging more girls to see themselves in STEM.

Takeaway

My biggest contribution was not a single mechanism. It was helping create a team that could design more thoughtfully, prototype with limited resources, perform at a higher competitive level, and bring younger students into engineering.

The experience taught me to connect technical decisions with people, schedules, documentation, and the realities of building a reliable system under competition pressure.
