Lithium-Ion Half-Cell Optimization

I investigated how the ratio of N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) to other electrode components affects slurry coating quality and loading density in LMO and LTO half cells.

Research question

Commercial battery formulations are often proprietary, and battery cells begin degrading soon after manufacture. To support repeatable laboratory research, our team needed a reliable in-house electrode recipe that could provide a consistent baseline.

My project focused on finding effective NMP-to-solids ratios for lithium manganese oxide (LMO) cathodes and lithium titanate oxide (LTO) anodes in coin cell and pouch batteries. NMP acts as the solvent that allows the active material slurry to spread evenly across the current collector before drying.

Method

I varied the amount of NMP and the total slurry volume to evaluate two connected outcomes: how evenly the slurry coated the current collector and the loading density of the finished electrode.

  1. Prepare the slurry. Adjust the NMP ratio while targeting a thick, uniform consistency suitable for coating.
  2. Coat the collectors. Apply LMO slurry to aluminum mesh and LTO slurry to copper mesh.
  3. Dry the electrodes. Secure the coated mesh on a rack and use a drying oven to evaporate the NMP.
  4. Compare samples. Evaluate coating consistency and loading density across formulations and slurry quantities.

Results

The study identified separate coating ratios for the two electrode materials and a repeatable slurry quantity for the test area.

What I learned

This project gave me hands-on experience with controlled experimentation, electrode preparation, careful documentation, and translating technical work for a broader audience. I concluded the program by presenting the research methods and results.

The work also showed me how an apparently small process variable—solvent ratio—can directly affect repeatability and the usefulness of future experimental data.
